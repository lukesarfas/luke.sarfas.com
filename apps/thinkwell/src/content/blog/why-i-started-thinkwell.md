I have wanted to journal for years. I just never did it.

I knew the arguments. Journaling is one of those things that almost every productive, thoughtful person seems to recommend at some point, usually with a kind of quiet certainty that suggests they think it is obvious. I have read the studies. I have downloaded the apps. I have bought the notebooks. And then, every time, after a couple of days, I have stopped.

The reason was always the same, and it took me a while to be honest about it. I am output driven. If I cannot see the effect of something I am doing, I lose interest in doing it, no matter how often I am told it is good for me. Journaling, as it is usually framed, gives you almost nothing back. You write a paragraph, you close the page, and the only artefact is a paragraph you will probably never reread. The benefit is supposed to come from the act itself, invisibly, over time. That is a fine thing to believe in. It is not a thing that gets me to open the app on a Tuesday evening when I am tired.

So I stopped pretending I was going to journal the conventional way, and started thinking about what would actually work for me.

## What I wanted

I wanted something that gave me a reason to come back. Not a streak counter. Not a guilt trip. Something that built up, visibly, the more I used it. I wanted to be able to see my entries arranged, connected, returned to. I wanted to interact with them, not just file them. I wanted the journal itself to feel like it was becoming something, not just accumulating.

When I framed it that way, the problem stopped being about discipline and started being about design. The reason I was not journaling was not that I lacked the willpower. It was that nobody had built the version of journaling I would have used.

So I decided to build it.

## The rule I try to follow when I build things

Whenever I build something, I want to build something that I would actually use. If I would not open it, I do not see the point. It is easy to design for an imagined user, optimise for an imagined workflow, and end up with a thing that is technically impressive and personally untouched. The world has plenty of those.

ThinkWell is the opposite of that. It is journaling, designed by someone who could never get journaling to stick, for the specific reasons it never stuck. Every decision in the app gets measured against one question: would this make me want to open it tomorrow? If the answer is no, it does not go in.

## The name

My girlfriend Olivia helped me build the app. She also came up with the name. ThinkWell. It captures what the app is for better than anything I came up with, which is part of why I am telling you she named it. The other part is that she should get credit for the bits that are good.

## What this blog is for

This blog is going to be a place for two things. The first is what I am learning about journaling itself, as someone who is finally doing it, and as someone building a tool for it. The second is what I am learning about the app, what is working, what is not, and where it is going.

If you have ever wanted to journal and never managed to, I think you will recognise some of what I write here. ThinkWell might be the version that works for you too. If it is not, that is fine. I am building it for me first, and putting it out in case it helps anyone else who was stuck for the same reasons I was.
