title: 40 journaling prompts sorted by how you feel right now
date: 2026-05-07
excerpt: Prompts organised by mood, not by theme or life area. Find where you are, pick one, and write for ten minutes.
---

The best journal prompt is the one that fits how you actually feel right now — not a prompt filed under "career" or "relationships." This list is sorted by current mood. Find where you are. Pick one. Write for ten minutes.

## When you feel overwhelmed

1. What's the one thing, if it were handled, that would make everything else feel more manageable?
2. Write everything that's competing for your attention right now — just list it, no order required.
3. What's been on your mind the most in the last 48 hours?
4. If you had to hand off three of your current worries to someone else for a week, which would you choose?
5. What's the smallest thing you could do today that would actually help?
6. What does "not overwhelmed" look like for you? What's different about that day?
7. What's actually urgent right now, and what just feels urgent?
8. Who or what is adding to the weight, and who or what is lightening it?

## When you feel stuck

9. What would you do today if you knew you couldn't fail?
10. What are you avoiding, and what's the story you're telling yourself about why?
11. What did you used to care about that you've stopped making time for?
12. What's the next small step — the smallest possible version of moving forward?
13. What would a more decisive version of you do with this situation?
14. Is this stuck feeling new, or has it been here before? What shifted it last time?
15. Write the worst realistic outcome of the thing you're hesitating on. (How survivable is it?)
16. What are you waiting for permission to do?

## When you feel anxious

17. What's the thought that keeps coming back? Write it down exactly, in one sentence.
18. What's the most realistic outcome of what you're worried about?
19. What would you tell a close friend who was feeling exactly this?
20. What would need to be true for this to feel okay?
21. Write the worry as if it's already in the past. What happened? How did it resolve?
22. What do you actually control in this situation?
23. Is there something you're not saying that needs to be said?
24. What's one thing you know for certain right now, even if everything else feels uncertain?

## When things are going well

25. What's working right now that you don't want to take for granted?
26. What made today feel lighter than usual?
27. Who has made things easier recently, and have you told them?
28. What would you want to remember about this period a year from now?
29. What's a decision you made recently that you'd make again?
30. What's a quality you've developed that you didn't have five years ago?
31. What would you tell yourself from a year ago?
32. What's something you've stopped worrying about that used to feel enormous?

## When you're making a decision

33. Write both options as if you've already chosen each one. How does each version of the story feel a year later?
34. What's the fear behind each option?
35. Who would be affected by this decision, and how?
36. Which option will you be more glad you chose in ten years?
37. What does your gut say, and what is it basing that on?
38. What would you regret more: doing it, or not doing it?
39. What information would actually change your decision, if you had it?
40. If a close friend described your situation and asked what they should do, what would you tell them?

---

A few notes on using these:

**Don't pick the "right" one.** Pick the one you least want to answer. That's usually the one that's waiting to be written.

**Set a timer.** Ten minutes is enough. Removing the open-ended pressure makes it easier to start.

**Write past the obvious answer.** The first sentence is usually what you already know. The useful part starts when you run out of easy things to say.
